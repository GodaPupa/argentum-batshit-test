# Ferocity Recycling — initial deck development v0.1

**Status: nine exact 60-card Ferocity prototypes and nine exact 60-card no-Ferocity alternatives, constructed before randomized outcomes. None is a supported winning list yet.** Every main-deck name has a retrieved card record. Ferocity is admitted only to the project's verified-text prerelease research path; a common printing does not establish sanctioned Pauper availability on the retrieval date. The source and mechanical admission gates remain separate.

The finite search, sample allocations, policy revisions, advancement and stopping rules are in [the research protocol](protocols/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md). This document fixes the actual initial packages, explains the third-family choice and supplies prototype pilot guidance. It does not add another search round or allow list changes during frozen evaluation.

## What is actually fixed

The initial search is exactly the nine Ferocity lists below. Three slots go to each family so that each architectural hypothesis receives a low-, middle- and higher-support treatment. These are whole packages: the number of Ferocities is deliberately **not** the only variable. A better result for one package cannot by itself establish that its Ferocity count caused the difference.

Each Ferocity list has a corresponding independently eligible no-Ferocity development alternative. The alternatives collectively receive the same three-list and gameplay budget per family. Their complete differences appear in each comparator JSON under `exact_package_delta_from_candidate`. Selecting the best-developed family comparator is permitted by the protocol; selecting an intentionally weak comparator is not.

All lists are version `0.1`. JSON files contain exact counts and a canonical deck SHA-256; plain-text files contain importable main decks. `decks/DECK_MANIFEST.json` binds all 18 identities. Mainboard hashes cover sorted `count name` lines; this differs from the file hash, which also covers metadata. The initial search manifest generated by the project tool additionally binds the actual files.

## Current-card correction before the initial freeze

The initial drafts were amended **before any randomized outcome or search freeze** after the source audit retrieved Wizards' September 4, 2026 announcement, [On the Zeta Set in Pauper](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/announcements/on-the-zeta-set-in-pauper). The audited policy admits the SLZ commons on the applicable September platform dates, subject to the stated bans. This materially changes the available support pool. Earlier recollections excluding all SLZ cards are not used as premises.

The correction consumes existing slots: A3's original draft was replaced by a Grixis Strix/Thoughtcast package; C received two Dispatches in place of two Cast Downs; and B received bounded Cut Down packages. No fourth family, tenth initial Ferocity configuration or extra development allocation was created. The overwritten material was unaccepted drafting work with zero outcomes, not prior research evidence. All 18 final initial lists remain version 0.1 until the initial manifest is frozen.

## Family A — artifact-sacrifice control, Rakdos and Grixis

The reason to try Rakdos is access to Shaman, Familiar, artifact card flow and red reach with fewer colors than the sourced Grixis Shaman benchmark. The cost is losing blue's actual package, especially Thoughtcast and its source-backed threats. We have not assumed that replacing that package is an improvement: A3 therefore gives the blue package a direct candidate slot within the same Shaman/Familiar artifact family. The contemporary benchmark source is documented separately in [BENCHMARK_AUDIT.md](BENCHMARK_AUDIT.md).

A1 keeps four Myr Enforcers and four Refurbished Familiars. Two Ferocities share the support slots with two Toxins. Four Bargains, four Insights, Wellsprings, Lembas and Fountains build a functional sacrifice shell. Bargain gains seven life when an Enforcer is sacrificed because its mana value remains seven even if affinity reduced the mana actually paid. That life is purchased by sacrificing the creature; it is not a free retained threat.

A2 changes the clock: two Enforcers and a Lembas become three Bone Pickers. One Toxin becomes a third Ferocity. Bone Picker is a flying threat that can survive Shaman's nonflying damage, but its reduced cost requires an actual creature death that turn. This configuration has fewer artifacts, so Familiar/Enforcer costs must be recomputed from the battlefield after every sacrifice.

A3 is Grixis: four newly eligible Baleful Strixes, four Familiars, two Enforcers and three Shamans, with four Thoughtcasts and four Bargains. Four Ferocities test the initial upper dose, but two Toxins retain the competing lifelink/Clue function. Strix supplies an immediate card and a flying deathtouch body; Ferocity adds little to its existing combat keyword, so the extra investment must earn its keep through power and a successful return/entry. Twenty-one artifact lands provide twelve black, ten blue and ten red printed sources; eleven always enter tapped. That tempo and three-color requirement must be charged against blue's card flow. Munitions supplies a mana-consuming finish and removal, not automatic lethal from a pile of spare artifacts.

**Critical friendly-fire distinction:** deathtouch Shaman damage kills the deck's own nonflying creatures, including otherwise large Enforcers. Familiar and Bone Picker have flying. A Shaman returned by Ferocity is tapped, unenchanted and a new object. Further already-pending activations may kill it again, without another Ferocity return. A pilot must inspect the entire proposed sequence before paying sacrifices.

Toxin's Clue is real artifact fodder for Shaman as well as a possible later draw. Spending the Clue as fodder forfeits its draw. Its lifelink applies to actual damage, including damage to friendly creatures; source controller, damage prevention and simultaneous outcomes must all be correct. Not Dead After All has a different return mechanism and a Wicked Role; it is not a one-mana spelling of Ferocity.

## Family B — black-based Crypt Rats attrition

B1 asks whether the best use of Ferocity is a **single** copy in an otherwise coherent black control shell. All 21 lands can produce black mana. Rats, Troll, monarch, lifegain artifacts, efficient removal and eight sacrifice-draw spells can function without drawing the Aura. The final pre-freeze draft uses two newly eligible Cut Downs in place of two of its three Defiles, retaining four Cast Downs for larger creatures. One small Ferocity package should not require constructing the entire deck around it. The price is a modest creature count and a slow finish, which the interactive engine must measure honestly.

B2 earns green with a complete package: four Malevolent Rumbles, two Avenging Hunters and one Generous Ent, plus an actual eleven-green-source mana base. Rumble can select lands, artifacts, threats, Rats **or Ferocity** because Ferocity is a permanent. It creates an Eldrazi **Spawn**, not a Scion. The Spawn can be sacrificed for colorless mana or to a draw spell, but its mana cannot pay Crypt Rats' black-only X cost. The ten always-tapped lands are an explicit tempo risk, not an invisible concession.

B3 changes B2 by replacing two Toxins and a Lembas with a third Ferocity and two Pulses of Murasa. Pulse can recover a creature or land while gaining life, but costs three mana and needs a legal target. This configuration tests whether that separately paid life buffer is competitive with Toxin's actual lifelink and Clue package. A successful Rats return does not cancel damage to either player or restore the life spent on Snuff Out.

The final B2 removal package is three Cast Downs, two Cut Downs and one Snuff Out. B3 uses three Cast Downs, one Cut Down and two Snuff Outs, using its Pulse life buffer to investigate the additional free-removal slot. Cut Down's power-plus-toughness restriction is real; it is not interchangeable with Cast Down against large threats. These are prospectively declared package differences, not post-outcome adjustments.

Crypt Rats damages **each creature and each player**. Flying is no protection. It may legally win by player damage, lose its controller the game, or produce a draw under the rules. Those results follow from life totals and actual simultaneous effects, never from a heuristic that treats a board wipe as victory.

The source audit caught a recent 61-card published list; that list must not be silently described as an exact 60 or converted by dropping an arbitrary card.

## Family C — why Orzhov Aura recovery was selected

This family was selected from the rules/resource audit before experimental outcomes. It asks whether recovering the spent Aura as a permanent, while keeping an evasive ETB-value plan, creates a practical advantage. It does not merely merge the red Shaman and green Rats cards into a three-color pile.

An Auramancer enchanted by Ferocity can be sacrificed to Insight. Ferocity's own trigger returns Auramancer tapped. The Aura has already gone to the graveyard through state-based actions, so the new Auramancer's entry trigger can target it. If those triggers resolve, Ferocity returns to hand and Insight eventually draws two. Starting with Auramancer already in play, this line costs **four mana**: two for Ferocity and two for Insight. The Aura is recovered and Auramancer is preserved; Insight is spent. It is a renewable source of value only when another draw spell and fresh mana are available. Flashing back Insight makes the corresponding cycle cost seven mana and exiles Insight afterward. Graveyard interaction and target removal can interrupt it. The detailed legal sequence is in [SYNERGY_MAP.md](SYNERGY_MAP.md).

The rest of C is a legitimate flying value plan: Familiar, Skyfisher and Hawk; artifacts that provide cards, life or removal; Inspector's Clue; and white exile removal. All three final initial drafts include two newly eligible Dispatches in place of two Cast Downs. Metalcraft must be checked at resolution after actual sacrifices and bounces; an early Dispatch without three artifacts does not exile a creature. Skyfisher can reuse an artifact or Omen, but its return trigger is mandatory. Hawk needs an artifact to return or it is sacrificed. Those costs must be charged, and bouncing a land may delay the next expensive spell. There is no creature sweeper in these initial C lists. Ferocity's deathtouch role is combat, with protection and recursion as its principal package hypotheses.

C1 begins with two Ferocities and two Auramancers. C2 trades one Hawk and one Wellspring for a third copy of each recovery piece. C3 trades its remaining two Wellsprings for a fourth Ferocity and second Omen. The recovery package therefore competes directly with artifact velocity, immediate cards and evasive pressure. No performance is assumed from the appealing interaction.

**The no-Ferocity C alternatives remove every Auramancer.** Omen and Journey remain when they serve the ETB/removal plan, but those few cards do not justify leaving three-mana recovery creatures behind merely to preserve a cosmetic comparison.

These broader comparisons answer whether the complete Ferocity/recovery package improves the shell. They cannot isolate the causal contribution of Ferocity alone from Auramancer and the other changed slots.

## Card-pool boundaries and omissions

The initial eligible pool is exactly the union of the 18 listed main decks. The mechanical audit may inspect other cards to understand alternatives; inspection does not authorize another initial configuration. During the single permitted refinement round, use at most two prospectively declared package variants per advancing candidate and the equivalent two-variant opportunity for its comparator, within the protocol budget. Complete new lists, including mana and policies, must be frozen before that round's outcomes. No family is added after a poor result.

| Audited direction | Decision before outcomes | Concrete reason or unresolved cost |
|---|---|---|
| Myr Retriever | Not in the initial nine | Two mana buys a 1/1 that returns another artifact to hand, which must then be recast; it does not recover Ferocity. Existing Fountain/Insight/Bargain packages and actual threats get the initial slots |
| Glaze Fiend | No initial slot; not admitted as a fallback card | Its temporary pump depends on continued artifact entries and does not provide an independent stable clock. Bone Picker is the selected evasive hypothesis; this is a design judgment, not a tested rejection |
| Evolution Witness | Audited, not in initial nine | Casting for three and adapting for two precedes the first permanent recovery; recasting Ferocity costs another two. A returned Witness has no old counters, but it still needs new mana and a new counter event to recover a card |
| Ashnod's Altar with Auramancer | No initial slot; no infinite-loop claim | A sacrifice produces two colorless mana; replaying Ferocity still needs a black or green mana each cycle. Available colorless mana does not satisfy the hybrid symbol |
| A Jund combination of A and B | Not selected as family C | Adds a third color and overlapping sacrifice/sweeper plans. The distinct recoverable-Aura question earned the third slot instead |
| Shambling Ghast and additional small death creatures | Audited; no final initial slot | The Ghast draft gave way to the current Strix/Thoughtcast hypothesis inside the same nine-list budget. A death trigger alone is insufficient compensation for lower threat quality and the extra two-mana Aura investment |
| Mandatory four Ferocities | Rejected as a search constraint | One- and two-copy treatments exist, mixed Toxin packages receive actual slots, and zero-copy alternatives receive equal development budgets |
| Deadly Dispute / Kuldotha Rebirth | Excluded by current legality audit | Neither is smuggled into an otherwise familiar older artifact list |

These omissions are bounded design choices. They do not establish that the omitted card could never support a better deck.

## Prototype mulligans and gameplay priorities

This is practical guidance for implementing and qualifying family-specific policies, **not yet a trained or frozen autonomous gameplay policy**. The protocol grants candidates and benchmark pilots equal bounded development opportunities. No policy may use an opponent's hidden hand or future draws.

**All families:** prefer a keep with working colored mana and at least one actual early development or interaction sequence. Two Ferocities plus no creature is not an engine hand. A hand with an Aura and a creature still needs the mana to deploy both and survive response windows. London-mulligan bottom decisions should remove redundant support or expensive cards before the only castable early action, with the matchup and visible information governing the decision. Do not mandate a Ferocity in every keep or mulligan away ordinary functional value hands solely because the Aura is absent.

**A:** a typical functional opening has two or three lands, black and red access, and artifact setup plus a draw spell or affordable threat. Compute affinity from permanents that will actually be in play after costs. A single-land hand needs a separately qualified exception; a pile of draw spells that each require sacrificing the only land is not automatically keepable. Lead tapped Drossforge Bridge when that best preserves the intended next-turn plays. Against pressure, holding up Toxin/Shaman or removal may matter more than immediately making a large Enforcer. If a sweep is needed, evaluate friendly nonflyers, Clue/fodder expenditure and the post-sweep clock.

For A3 specifically, a keep whose only early threat is Strix needs a credible blue-and-black sequence. Two tapped Bridges do not make a turn-two Strix. Plan the land sequence and color priorities explicitly; do not treat Thoughtcast's affinity as removing its blue requirement. Strix already has deathtouch, so merely enchanting it does not add another independent deathtouch benefit.

**B:** prioritize black access, an early removal or artifact/card-flow play, and a plausible path to Rats or a real threat. A one-land hand plus Troll still needs a land that can pay for swampcycling; this is selection, not ramp, and consumes the Troll. In B2/B3, decide which tapped green/black land enables the actual second and third turns. Count black mana separately from total mana when planning Rats. Do not take the initiative or monarch into an undefended board merely because the entry ability draws future resources. Keep a life buffer for the opponent's plausible public racing threats and any planned Rats activations; calculate the actual damage rather than assuming recursion stabilizes.

**C:** prefer working white and black access plus a castable ETB sequence. Do not keep Hawk as a one-mana threat when there is no artifact to return, and charge Skyfisher's mandatory bounce. An artifact-land bounce can preserve a creature but set development back a turn. Auramancer without a recoverable enchantment is ordinarily a low-priority three-drop. Use Ferocity on Familiar or Auramancer when the preserved body and resolved trigger justify its two mana; preserving a tapped creature does not provide a same-turn blocker. Deathtouch in combat is useful only if damage is actually dealt and the creature can legally participate.

**No-Ferocity versions:** use their own best lines and mulligans. Toxin can create fodder or life; Not Dead After All has a cheaper protection window and a Role; additional threats/removal/card-flow cards change sequencing. No pilot may pretend the removed Ferocity is still required for its normal game plan.

## Sideboards and eventual practical deliverables

Every initial JSON has an empty `sideboard` and `sideboard_status: not_yet_developed_or_qualified`. This is intentional stage labeling, not a claim that a tournament list can ignore its sideboard. No 60/15 has been selected or supported yet.

The later sideboard-development stage must use current legal source records and exact opposing 75s. It must cover racing, counterspells, graveyard hate, artifact hate and strategies that ignore creature sweepers; all swaps need real quantities and legal post-swap decks. It cannot alter a frozen main deck based on confirmation results. The eventual supported deck, if any, receives a full importable 60/15, tested sideboarding plans and a revised practical primer after the appropriate stopping rule. The present prototypes and guidance must remain visibly distinguished from that final deliverable.
